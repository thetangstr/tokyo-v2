NFLooker · Domain-specific explanation LM

Three endpoints.
Three rewards.
One cited answer.

NFLooker is Snapcount's small language model — purpose-built for NFL explanation, reasoning, and play-by-play narration. Not a chatbot. Not a wrapper around GPT. A fine-tuned Qwen 3.5 with three LoRA adapters, three citation mechanisms, and three reward heads — one of which trains itself against SnapScore's own prediction error.

§ 01 · Three endpoints

Three different questions. Three different mechanisms.

NFLooker serves at :8000 on Mac Studio. Every output cites its source and carries a confidence score. The endpoint determines the citation shape, the corpus, and the reward signal — they share a Qwen 3.5 trunk but are otherwise three distinct fine-tunes.

POST /api/nfloooker/explain

"Why did SnapScore project Mahomes for 287 yards?"

Takes a SnapScore projection + its supporting facts (SHAP top features, coach profile, injury status, Vegas line). Returns a plain-English explanation with per-claim citations back into the input facts.

GET /api/nfloooker/reason

"How does Andy Reid use Kelce in the red zone?"

Free-text NFL question. Returns a RAG-grounded answer with citations into a corpus of PBP + NGS + coach profiles + injury history + Vegas archives.

POST /api/nfloooker/simulate

"Narrate this drive."

Takes a game-state + a SnapSim decision trace (TB12's lane). Returns a narrative + per-step rationale that follows the trace's structure exactly — each output step maps to a snapsim_trace[step_id].

Hard rule · all three

Zero hallucinated claims is the floor

Citation enforcement is architectural, not a guideline. Each endpoint's output shape requires the citation mechanism to match the input shape — a model that skips citation can't produce a valid response.

§ 02 · Why a small model, not a frontier API

Three reasons we're not just wrapping GPT-5.

Reason 01 · Local + deterministic + fast

Mac Studio, <2s p95 latency on /explain

Runs on M2 Max 64GB at home. No rate limits, no rolling deprecations, no per-request cost. Snapcount agents call it over LAN at :8000 like any other internal service.

Reason 02 · The reward signal is ours

The /explain head trains against projection_error

SnapScore's MAE on QB passing yards is the reward signal for NFLooker's /explain head. Improving the explanation literally improves the underlying prediction — the LM becomes a feature engineer. You cannot do this against a closed API.

Reason 03 · Architectural citation

The shape forces grounding

Each endpoint's output schema requires the citation mechanism to match the input shape. /explain citations are JSON pointers into request.facts. /reason citations are RAG chunk IDs. /simulate citations are trace step IDs. A model that skips citation can't return a valid response.

What we explicitly skip

Pretraining + multimodal + DFS-coaching

We do not pretrain from scratch — fine-tune on top of Qwen 3.5. We do not handle image/audio in V1. We do not do DFS-coaching (mission creep — out of scope, that was the prior NFL_LLM lane which we ignore).

§ 04 · Training stages

Three stages, not one.

Most "fine-tune an LLM on NFL data" plans collapse training into a single SFT step. NFLooker has three distinct stages — and the third is where the value compounds.

Stage 01 · N1 · Corpus build

Three separate corpora

/explain (≥2K examples): built from ~/snapcount/snapscore-examples/. Every SnapScore projection + SHAP top-features becomes an example. Adam's weekly news packs supply the surrounding facts.

/reason RAG (≥1.5K examples): PBP + NGS + coach profiles + injury history + Vegas archives. Each Q&A includes the gold retrieval set so we can grade hit-rate directly.

/simulate (≥1K examples): decision-traces from TB12's SnapSim (or a minimal in-NFLooker generator if TB12 isn't ready). Each example forces narrative to reference each step_id.

Stage 02 · N2 · Supervised fine-tuning

Ship V1 at this gate

LoRA fine-tune Qwen 3.5 with three adapters on the shared trunk. Three live endpoints at :8000 with citations and confidence scores. This is what enables the cascading checks — once V1 is serving, the RL stage can use the served model's outputs as its training environment.

Stage 03 · N4 · RL post-training

Where the value compounds

Three different reward heads, one per endpoint. This is the architecturally important part — and the part most fine-tuning plans miss. See § 05 for the three reward signals. A unified projection_error reward across all three endpoints would collapse /reason quality; the multi-head design was the resolution of round 2 of the deep-interview.

§ 05 · The 3 reward heads

The closed loop on projection_error.

The /explain reward signal is what makes this project structurally different from "fine-tune an LM on football text." Improving the LM literally improves SnapScore.

EndpointReward signalWhy this signal
/explainClosed loop projection_error delta — the LM's chain-of-thought is parsed into structured features which get fed back into SnapScore. If SnapScore's MAE drops, the LM gets reward. The LM becomes a feature engineer. This is the structural moat: improving the explanation improves the underlying prediction.
/reasonRubric LLM-judge rubric — factual accuracy + citation faithfulness graded by an independent evaluator RAG quality has a weak gradient on projection_error, so we use a rubric-based reward instead. Unified-reward attempt would collapse this endpoint's quality.
/simulateTrace SnapSim outcome-distribution alignment — does the narrated drive end up in the same outcome distribution SnapSim's Monte Carlo produces? Constrains the narrative to be consistent with the actual coach-agent decision physics. Cross-checks against TB12's pillar 3 work.

§ 09 · Where this fits

The third pillar.

The modeling lane's finding from M5: the path to MAE 25 / sMAPE <15% is new signal, not new models. NFLooker is both. The /explain RL loop is a mechanism for the LM to discover new features by reasoning about projections out loud — and those features feed back into SnapScore. It's the long-term moat — in-house generative narrative grounded in real predictions — and it compounds with every weekly data pack Adam delivers.

SnapScore predicts the number. SnapSim coaches the play. NFLooker explains why, in plain English, with citations.
