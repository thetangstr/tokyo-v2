Ch. 01The Goal2023

Build the most accurate fantasy football engine — with no humans in the loop.

Every existing projection system leaned on a person. An analyst tweaked a number. An editor overrode a call. We wanted a machine that could run the whole season without anyone touching it — and still beat the humans who used to touch it.

V1Analyze the analysts

Reverse-engineer the pros

We spent the first month breaking down how top analysts actually built projections. Spreadsheets. Gut calls. Hand-weighted last-4-game averages. We listed everything they did and asked: which of these could a machine do better, and which would a machine miss?

Two answers emerged, and they became our Big Rocks.

First principles
V2The Big Rocks

End-to-end simulation · Sequential snap prediction

Big Rock #1 — End-to-end game simulation. Humans can't re-run a game 1,000 times. Machines can. A real projection isn't a number; it's a distribution over a thousand alternate realities.

Big Rock #2 — Sequential snap prediction. Stats are outcomes of plays, and plays depend on everything that happened before them. Game script drives volume. Volume drives stats. Sequence matters.

Everything we built after this pointed back at these two rocks.

Big Rocks identified
Ch. 02First PrinciplesEarly 2024

EPA as north star. The Time Machine Rule.

Before we could predict anything, we had to decide what "good" meant. And we had to invent a rule that would save us from the single most common failure mode in sports ML.

V3EPA everywhere

Expected Points Added becomes the unit of measurement

Yards lie. Touchdowns are lumpy. EPA — the change in expected points a play produces — stayed honest. We refactored every feature around it. Efficiency over volume. Quality over surface stats.

North star metric
V4Time Machine Rule

Never use data the model couldn't have had

Our first backtests looked incredible. 8% error. We almost celebrated. Then we found it: one feature was looking at end-of-game snap counts to predict that same game's yards. A perfect score and a perfectly worthless model.

We codified the Time Machine Rule: every feature must be re-creatable from only the information that existed before kickoff. No look-ahead. No leakage. No exceptions. Every calculator has to prove it.

Hard-earned ruleConstitutional
The first backtest that looks too good is always a leak. The second is usually a leak too. If the number makes you happy, audit the feature that most wants to explain it. — Internal lesson, V4 postmortem
V5Monolith ships

One model, all positions, four stats. It worked — barely.

V5 was our first real end-to-end model. One XGBoost regressor, sixty-ish features, every position. It beat the industry on QB yards and lost to the mean on TE yards. It was the first thing we could point at and say: "that's a projection system."

It was also the last monolith we ever shipped.

First production model
Ch. 03Layered ThinkingMid 2024

The monolith cracked. A 5-layer architecture took its place.

V5 worked for QBs because QBs are largely about volume and scheme. It broke for running backs because rushing depends on what the defense is doing. One model couldn't carry that load. We split it.

V6–V105-layer architecture

Features organized by what they describe, not when they were added

We stopped piling features into a flat list. Instead: five layers, each answering a different question. A feature can only belong to one layer. Every layer has a review gate.

The 5-Layer Feature Architecture
L1 · Context
Game script, weather, rest days, travel, short-week penalty. The situational scaffolding every play inherits.
12 features
L2 · Vegas
Implied totals, spreads, line movement. A market consensus we use as prior — then we try to beat it.
14 features
L3 · Team Process
EPA per dropback, success rate, neutral pace, pass-over-expected. How the team actually generates value.
28 features
L4 · Tracking
Air yards, separation, pressure rate, time-to-throw. What the on-field chips actually recorded.
29 features
L5 · Player Process
Route participation, YPRR, TPRR, pressure evasion. Usage-normalized signal.
32 features

Total: 115 process calculators by the time V10 shipped. Every one traceable to a layer. Every one Time-Machine-Rule-verified. Every one reviewed by a human and a constitutional checker before it landed.

Ch. 04The ML AwakeningLate 2024

Transformers. Reinforcement learning. The T13RL loop.

Tabular models hit a ceiling. Adding feature 116 didn't help the way adding feature 16 had. We needed architectures that could learn sequence and strategy, not just correlations.

V11The Constitutional Rule

No feature can be a historical average of the stat being predicted

We found eight calculators quietly regressing to last season's averages. We banned the entire pattern. Backtest accuracy dropped 3%. Forward prediction improved 5% within two weeks. The best rule we ever wrote is the one that makes the tests look worse.

Discipline = moatStill enforced today
V12Transformer exploration

Sequence models enter the chat

We tokenized drives. Each snap became a token. The model saw [SNAP][DOWN_2][DIST_7][SHOTGUN][PASS][12_YDS] instead of a row in a table. Attention learned which earlier snaps mattered for predicting the next one. Not production-ready yet, but the signal was undeniable.

Sequence > table
V13T13RL system

Triple-reward reinforcement learning

V13 was the first version to decide rather than just predict. The T13RL system fine-tunes the model against three rewards simultaneously: accuracy on realized outcomes, calibration of its own confidence, and agreement with Vegas market movement. The model has to get all three right.

T13RL · Triple-Reward Loop
Reward 1 · Accuracy
Was the prediction close to the actual outcome? Penalize error, reward closeness.
Reward 2 · Calibration
Did the model's confidence match reality? 80% predictions must hit 80% of the time.
Reward 3 · Market
Did our prediction agree with Vegas line movement? Divergence = sharp edge or bad signal.
The model has to satisfy all three — not average them, not pick one.
Ch. 05The ReckoningNov 2025

V14 shipped. Then we audited it. Then we rewrote the project.

V14 was supposed to be the win. 115 features. MAE training. Walk-forward validation. Dashboards. And then we ran a clean holdout and everything fell apart.

V14The audit that saved us

0-for-4 on Golden Metrics. The features added 1.1pp over the mean.

In November 2025 we ran a proper 2024 holdout — weeks 9 through 18 — with zero training overlap. The results ended an 18-month winning streak of optimistic dashboards:

White mark = Golden Metric target. Every position missed. The full feature set beat predicting the mean by 1.1 percentage points.
Every success claim we'd made for six months was an evaluation artifact. The model wasn't predicting stats — it was memorizing them. We shipped twenty versions built on a mismeasured baseline. V14 is where we stopped lying to ourselves. — V14 postmortem, internal

What V14 taught us, that V1 through V13 couldn't: NFL stats have ~28% coefficient of variation game-to-game. A single number — however well-engineered — will always lose to a distribution. The answer wasn't "better features." The answer was "stop predicting single numbers."

Ch. 06Sequence & ContextMid 2025

Gridiron-X. A context-augmented transformer.

V15 was the first full rewrite. We kept the 115 features as inputs, but the prediction head was a transformer that read tokenized sequences — player context, Vegas context, play sequence, prediction targets — and attended to what mattered.

V15Gridiron-X

Tokenize everything. Let attention decide what matters.

Every pre-game state became a sequence:

<GAME_START> <WEEK_7> <HOME_KC> <AWAY_SF>
<SPREAD_TOSS> <TOTAL_HIGH> <IMPLIED_HIGH_KC>
<ROSTER> <Mahomes> <QB> <EPA_ELITE> <CPOE_ELITE> <HEALTHY>
<Kelce> <TE> <WOPR_ELITE> <TARGET_HIGH> <QUESTIONABLE> ...
<PREDICT> <Mahomes> → [regression head]

The transformer didn't need us to tell it what features mattered. Attention figured it out. And because the inputs were tokens, we could keep adding context — weather, matchup history, travel — without re-engineering the feature table.

Context tokenization
Ch. 07Simulate the GameLate 2025

V16: stop predicting a number. Simulate the game 1,000 times.

V14's lesson ("single numbers lose") and V15's architecture ("sequences work") met in V16. Instead of predicting Mahomes's yards, we simulated Mahomes's game — every snap, every choice — a thousand times. Then we read the distribution.

V16Distributions, not numbers

Multi-agent coordinator simulation

Three agents started calling plays at each other: an offensive coordinator persona, a defensive coordinator persona, and a statistical resolver that computed the outcome of each snap from a distribution calibrated on 300K real plays. Mahomes stopped being a number. He became a probability shape — thin on the low end, fat around the median, long on the upside.

Multi-agent sim
V16.5AutoResearch comes online

40 autonomous experiments per night

We gave the system a frozen evaluation oracle, a constitutional checker, and the keys to the feature codebase. Every night it ran forty experiments against itself — new feature combinations, new model architectures, new loss functions. Improvements got kept. Regressions got reverted.

The Poisson breakthrough that halved our TD error? Experiment 23 at 2 AM. No human saw it until the morning.

Flywheel
Ch. 08V17 · The Synthesis2026 — Today

Three engines. One feedback loop.

V17 is the first version where every prior lesson is load-bearing. The 115 process features. The Time Machine Rule. The 5-layer architecture. T13RL. Tokenized sequences. Multi-agent simulation. Autonomous research. None of it is bolted on. All of it feeds itself.

V17Current build

Process + Form + Chess, fused

PARIS (the ML engine, built from V5–V14's 115-calculator stack) predicts from eight years of history. DENVER (the AutoResearch loop, born in V16.5) runs experiments while we sleep. TOKYO (the multi-agent simulation, the descendant of V16) plays the game a thousand times.

The simulation discovers features no human would engineer. Those features go into PARIS. The improved PARIS makes DENVER's experiments sharper. DENVER finds new architectures for TOKYO. Around and around.

Goal for the 2025 season: <10% Percentage RMSE on our Golden Metrics. The target the project was founded on, seventeen builds ago.

The lesson seventeen versions taught us.

Football projection isn't a feature-engineering problem. It isn't a model-selection problem. It's a discipline problem — keep the rules, audit yourself, and let the system rebuild itself faster than your competitors can rebuild theirs. That's V17. That's the moat.
